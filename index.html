Why Solana

  
One network that settles in under a second for a fraction of a cent, already used for payments by large financial companies.

  

    
$1.9Tstablecoin volume in H1 202622.5% of global stablecoin volume
$16.7Bstablecoins issued on SolanaJuly 2026
120Mtransactions per dayQ3 2026 average
30+ monthswithout a network outagelast outage February 2024

  

  

    
Settles in under a second
Transactions confirm in about 0.4 seconds, so your server can mark an order paid before the customer leaves the page.
Costs a fraction of a cent
A flat base fee of about $0.001 whatever the amount. Your app can pay it, so users only ever hold dollars.
Compliance controls in the token
Token-2022 extensions add allowlists, freezing and pause controls that apply in every wallet and app.
One network, no bridges
Every app shares the same state and liquidity. There are no rollups or bridges to integrate.

Design notes
Why this page looks the way it does. These are for reviewers, not part of the product. Full evidence and sources: Docs Research.


WhyThe page has one job: get a developer into the right path within one scroll. The order is hero (with a code example) → job picker → Why Solana → cost calculator → guided routes. There's no standalone concepts section.
WhyWhy Solana follows Tempo's pattern of a few sourced stat tiles, plain benefit lines and named production users. Every figure has a source link and an as-of date. Check them against Artemis or the Foundation's own data before launch.
Vs. todayToday's hero buttons are Read the core docs and Browse templates, and the use-case grid is the third block, below Learn the stack / Build by example / Move to Solana. Here the primary button scrolls to the picker, and the picker is the only block with green buttons.
WhyThe quiet links under the hero (Go to the docs, Coming from Ethereum) keep today's crypto-native developers one click from where they go now. The redesign is aimed at web2 developers, and it shouldn't cost the current users anything.
Risk / dependencyKeep the network numbers honest. The page shows only sourced figures (Why Solana) and computed comparisons (the cost calculator), with no live or simulated transaction data. If a live module is added later, serve it from an indexer-backed endpoint rather than public RPC in the browser, which is slow and rate-limited. (a) TPS excludes validator votes, because raw TPS is inflated about 3× and skeptical developers will notice. (b) “Under a second” is true for confirmed; finalized is ~13 s unless Alpenglow ships first, so label it. (c) A dollar fee needs a price feed, with a fallback to SOL.
WhyEach job card follows the template: the developer's question as the title, one line on fit that compares against something they already know (card fees, a database), and the time to a first result. The cards name no concepts.
Vs. todayProducts are gone from this page. The 8-product grid becomes part of the paths: Commerce Kit in Payments, the x402 facilitator and Kora in Pay-per-call, Token ACL and Attestations in Tokenization. A grid of nine product names is a concept gate too.
Vs. todayNav: the Build dropdown now leads with What are you building? (hover it). Token Extensions moves out of the menu and into the tokenization job. DeFi and Gaming sit behind More use cases until their paths exist. Reorder the global nav only once the Payments path is live.
WhyInternet capital markets is the frame; the jobs stay in the developer's words. The headline uses the Foundation's narrative, and the very next sentence defines it in plain terms (money, assets and markets on the internet, open 24/7). The picker then splits it into building blocks: Money → payments, Pricing → pay-per-call, Assets → tokenization, with Markets behind them. Each card title is still the developer's own question.
Risk / dependencyCheck that web2 developers understand “internet capital markets”. It isn't chain vocabulary, but it is a coined term. Add one question to the MLH rerun script: “After reading the first screen, what do you think you could build here?” If people can't answer, keep ICM as the eyebrow and move the plain-language line into the headline.
WhyThe words “web3”, “blockchain”, “decentralized” and “keypair” still appear nowhere on this page.
